About Gambling Data

A running account of what Illinois loses to legal gambling and what the state does about it, compiled from public records by one clinician, free of industry money.

What this is

Illinois publishes more gambling data than almost any state: the Illinois Gaming Board reports every month, to the dollar, what was played and lost at every licensed video gaming location, casino and sportsbook, and the state’s forecasting commission and Auditor General publish the lottery’s figures and the taxes collected. What Illinois does not publish is any of it in one place, over time, with the products side by side and the definitions reconciled. These pages do that: all four legal products together, and one page each for video gambling, casinos and sports betting, with charts, full data tables, timelines, a plain account of what the numbers can and cannot say, and the sources and methods behind every figure.

The site started with Illinois because that is where its editor practises and where the public data is richest. Other states may follow as time allows; nothing here will be expanded faster than it can be checked.

Who compiles it

Chris Beerman, LCSW, CADC, is a clinical social worker in Chicago. He has worked in addiction treatment for about eight years, in hospital detox and residential programs before opening Chicago Addiction Therapy in 2022, a private practice in Logan Square that treats gambling disorder, sports betting, high-risk trading and alcohol problems, in person and by telehealth across Illinois and Minnesota. He holds a Master of Social Work from Loyola University Chicago, is licensed as a clinical social worker in Illinois (LCSW) and Minnesota (LICSW), is a Certified Alcohol and Drug Counselor, completed the Illinois Council on Problem Gambling’s 30-hour problem gambling counselor training, and is a member of the National Council on Problem Gambling. He also edits the Gambling Research Index, a nightly index of new gambling studies and policy news.

He keeps these pages because the numbers clients bring into the office, what a machine in a bar took in a month, what a sportsbook kept, how little of the tax goes back into treatment, deserve the same care as the clinical work, and because journalists, researchers and legislators kept asking for figures that were public but scattered.

Independence

This site takes no funding, advertising, data or affiliate income from gambling operators, suppliers, affiliates, lotteries, prediction-market exchanges or bodies they fund. It carries no advertising and no tracking beyond what the hosting provider logs: no analytics scripts, no cookies, no forms.

Interests to declare: the editor runs Chicago Addiction Therapy, a private practice that treats gambling disorder, and may benefit reputationally from this work. The pages link to that practice’s free tools and guides where a reader might need them. He has no relationship with any wagering operator, exchange, intermediary or exclusion service, and no industry body funds his practice, his research or this site.

Standards

  • Primary sources only. Every figure traces to a public record named on the page: Illinois Gaming Board monthly and annual reports, the Commission on Government Forecasting and Accountability’s Wagering in Illinois updates, the Auditor General’s lottery audits, Department of Human Services releases, and the state’s own statutes. Reported journalism is used only for figures the state does not publish, and is labelled as such.
  • Reconciled definitions. “Losses” means the same thing on every page: what players wagered minus what was paid back to them. Fiscal years run July to June and are marked. Figures computed from monthly reports are checked against the state’s own annual totals and the difference is stated when there is one.
  • Dated and reviewed. Each page shows the month its data runs through, the date it was last reviewed and its update schedule. Pages are refreshed after the Gaming Board posts new months, usually in the first week of the month.
  • Corrections in the open. An error is corrected on the page with a dated note in the methodology section, not quietly.
  • Gaps named. Where Illinois publishes no data, the page says so rather than estimating around it. There is no statewide prevalence study newer than 2021, no annual count of people treated for gambling disorder and no helpline series, and the pages say that next to the figures that do exist.
  • Activity is not harm. Revenue and loss figures measure gambling activity and the state’s response to it, not the number of people with a gambling problem. The pages do not equate growth in losses with growth in addiction.
  • How it is written. The editor compiles the data, writes and checks the pages, and uses AI assistance for drafting, charting and arithmetic under his review. Errors are his. The practice’s fuller editorial standards and clinical review policy apply here too.

How the pages are made

The Illinois Gaming Board’s reporting portals publish monthly figures for video gaming (by location and municipality, since September 2012), casinos (by casino, since July 2020 in the current format) and sports wagering (by licensee, since March 2020). Those tables are downloaded, summed into statewide monthly, calendar-year and fiscal-year series, and checked against the Gaming Board’s own annual reports and the forecasting commission’s annual update. Lottery figures come from the Auditor General’s audited financial statements and the commission’s estimates for the year not yet audited. Per-adult figures divide by about 10.1 million adult residents, the Census Bureau’s Vintage 2025 estimate for Illinois less the share under 18, the same denominator on every page. Each page’s methodology section gives the specific sources, definitions and known limitations for that product.

Corrections and contact

A wrong figure, a broken link, a newer source: write to chris@chicagoaddictiontherapy.com or open an issue in the repository. Corrections are made on the page with a dated note.

If you need help with gambling

This site is for data. If gambling is causing harm to you or someone close to you, the Illinois helpline is 1-800-GAMBLER (call) or text GAMB to 833234, free and around the clock; nationally, 1-800-MY-RESET. In a crisis, call or text 988. The editor’s practice, Chicago Addiction Therapy, offers a free three-minute screening, a 24-hour betting shutdown plan, a guide to Illinois self-exclusion, and therapy for gambling in person in Chicago and online across Illinois and Minnesota.
